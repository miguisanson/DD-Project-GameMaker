# Balance report (packet E1: simulator, targets, proposals)

> **D111 and D112 are applied (section 6).** Sections 0-5 were written on the data of 2026-10-14, before the owner approved E1-1 .. E1-6; their "today" is the game before D111 and their proposals are now in the game. E1-7 (Mage HP die) and E1-8 (economy) were not approved.

Generated by `python tools/balance/report.py` (data build `5bf4580-20261014`, seed 20261014, 400 runs per class x profile cell). Nothing in the game was changed (D13): every number below that differs from the game is a **proposal** the owner approves in a batch.

## 0. Read this first

- **Method.** `tools/balance/sim.py` re-implements the combat spec (damage, statuses, SPD timeline, stagger/BREAK, B4 enemy rule lists, wind-ups and boss phases), the C2 card offers and level-ups, the C3 gold, shops, elites and loot, on the data the game exports (`-exportdata`). The formulas are imported from `check_combat_vectors.py`; `sim.py --vectors` replays all 137 combat vectors plus the exported class and enemy tables through the simulator (144 checks, all pass). `test_sim.py` has 21 more checks.
- **Players.** QTE skill profile (Perfect/Good/OK/Weak): novice 15/35/35/15, average 30/40/25/5, expert 60/30/10/0. Decisions: the *greedy* policy (documented in `sim.py`, class `Policy`): heals before a killing blow, throws a bomb at hard fights, spends MP where it buys most, takes the card with the best expected gain, buys by value per gold. A *basic-attack-only* policy brackets it from below (section 1.6). A real first-time player sits between the two.
- **Not modelled** (so the numbers are optimistic for the player): walking into extra fights, fleeing, equipment passives that need state (Sunder Edge, Pinning Shot, Mirror Stitch), selling items, the echo gift, and any mistake in reading a wind-up. Foresight's information is not used by the policy, so Foresight looks weak here.
- **Targets** are in `tools/balance/targets.json` (from D14/D30/D61, balance-methods 6.2, combat-spec section 7 and the packet brief). One reading call: Nobody is the pure challenge class (D83), so it has its own win-rate band (average 15-30%) and is left out of the +-10-point class spread and the fight-length bands; put it back in `class_spread_classes` / `fight_turn_classes` for the literal brief.
- **Run length is a lower bound.** Time = (player + enemy actions) x 2.5 s + 1.5 s per defence bar + 25 s per floor + 6 s per fight + 8 s per card pick + 45 s per shop + 150 s story, as the brief says. Rooms are 16 x 11 tiles (one screen), the map has 19 free enemies plus 6 fixed fights.

## 2. Findings

1. **The game is far too easy and far too short for the plan.** Greedy Knight / Archer / Mage win 90-100% of runs at every skill profile (target 35-55%), the median winning run is about 20 minutes (target 45-60), a floor drains 18-32% of HP (advisory target 60-80%) and a whole run uses 0.2-1.0 HP potions. Gold is not a decision: about 600 of the 750 earned is left at the end.
2. **Elites and bosses are too short.** Elites last about as long as a normal fight; Palehorn 5-7 turns; the Pale Choirbeast 3.7 turns for a Mage, 6-7 for the others.
3. **SPD decides the class order.** The Knight (SPD 8) gets 0.67 actions per Palehorn action, the Archer (SPD 13+) 1.3. A fast Archer or any multi-hit user also breaks the boss meter before the wind-up lands (BREAK cancels it, D75), so in the traces the Archer rarely eats a heavy hit: it wins 99-100% at every skill profile while the Knight loses at Palehorn (8 of 400 average runs).
4. **Class gap.** Mage: strongest burst (Fireball burn is 12 damage on top of the hit) and the squishiest at Palehorn. Nobody: no skills, weakest growth; it stalls on Palehorn (17-18 player turns) and survives only through throwables.
5. **The MP economy is one passive.** MP does not regenerate between fights, a Knight has 11-20 MP, skills cost 3-5. Mana Spring (+1 MP every own turn) is the highest-valued passive for Knight and Archer (gain 64 and 49 HP-equivalents, the next passive 36) because it is the only MP source; a winning run ends with only 1.3-1.5 of its 3 skill slots filled, the first skill card is almost always taken and the rest lose to passives.
6. **Bug, found while porting Skill_AdjustStatusTurns:** a status with a fixed `duration` ignores the skill's own turns, so **Foresight+ (+2 turns) does nothing** (stays 3). Muscle Up+/Rev Up+/Evasion+/Take Aim+/Meditation+ are fine (those statuses have no fixed duration). Not a balance number: a one-line fix in `Skill_AdjustStatusTurns` (use `max(fixed, skill turns)` or put `status_turns` on the upgrade into `duration`).
7. **Doc slip:** `combat-spec.md` section 11 prints the order for SPD 13 vs 9 as `P E P P E P P E P`; the gauge rule, the reference (`turn_13_9`) and the game give `P E P E P P E P E`.

### 4.10 What the numbers cannot fix (needs an owner decision, not a number)

- **Run length.** The plan says 45-60 minutes; the map holds 19 free enemies plus Palehorn, 3 Strangers and the Choirbeast on one-screen rooms. Longer fights add a few minutes; the other 25 need content (more enemies per room, enemy respawn, optional rooms) or a shorter promise. D28 forbids new content, so this needs a grill-me.
- **Archer.** Even with every proposal the Archer wins 90-96% at every skill profile: SPD plus BREAK-cancelled wind-ups let it avoid the heavy hits that defence bars are meant to price. Options: boss meters that refill after a cancel, a wind-up that cannot be cancelled twice in a row, or heavy moves that hit before the first player turn. All are rule changes.
- **Gold.** Numbers alone only trade income for prices; the game needs either more things to buy (cards, rerolls, removal, as in the research) or less gold.
- **Foresight** needs its information used before it can be judged (the sim does not read wind-up previews).
